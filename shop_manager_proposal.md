# Proposal: Shop Manager Role & Digital Integration
**Submitted by:** Patrick "PJ" Losey
**Objective:** To officially transition into the Shop Manager position and integrate UpfittersOS into daily operations, driving efficiency, accountability, and profitability on the shop floor.

## 1. Executive Summary & Vision
To maximize the efficiency and profitability of the shop floor, the Shop Manager role must be deeply integrated with modern operational software. By building and deploying **UpfittersOS**, we will enforce strict Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), eliminate wasted time, and provide unparalleled visibility into job progress.

This creates a dual-value proposition: 
1. A highly optimized, data-driven SAE shop floor today.
2. A battle-tested software product that can eventually be packaged and sold as a SaaS solution to other upfitters nationwide.

## 2. Floor Operations & "The Gatekeeper" Model
A primary goal is to keep technicians in their bays turning wrenches, rather than wandering the floor searching for parts or answers.

* **Morning Meetings:** I will lead the daily morning meetings for the upfitting staff to set expectations, address roadblocks, and outline the day's targets.
* **The Parts Liaison:** The Shop Manager acts as the sole middleman between the upfitters and the parts department. Techs should never need to leave the bay to track down inventory.
* **Pre-Staging & Deadline Management:** I will utilize the software to ensure vehicles are prepped (e.g., CompanyCam pre-inspections completed, initial parts staged) *before* the tech clocks into the job. This oversight keeps deadlines on track and formally logs exactly why a deadline was missed (e.g., waiting on parts, tech pulled off for a rush job).

## 3. Quality Control (QC) & Accountability
Every job must pass through a strict, software-enforced pipeline to guarantee quality and accurate billing.

* **Time Tracking vs. Book Time:** Techs must clock in and out of specific tasks within UpfittersOS. This data will be reviewed by the Shop Manager and VP (Paul) to verify that actual time aligns with book time, allowing for data-driven adjustments.
* **The QC Pipeline:** 
    1. Tech completes a task and sets its status to **"Ready for QC."**
    2. Shop Manager is notified and inspects the work against defined SOPs. *(Note: Initial QC phase will involve shadowing Paul to learn and establish these standards).*
    3. **QC Kickbacks:** If issues are found, the job is kicked back to the tech with notes. The tech must clock *back* onto the task, and this time will be specifically flagged as "Work Done After QC" for our records.
    4. **Final QC:** Once all individual tasks on a vehicle are marked as QC Passed, the Manager performs one comprehensive "Final QC" on the vehicle before it is ever marked "Ready for Customer" or pickup is scheduled.

## 4. Chain of Command & Issue Resolution
To maintain morale and a highly focused environment, a strict chain of command must be enforced regarding floor issues.

* **Management Buffer:** When the VP or Upper Management identifies an issue on the floor, it must be brought directly to the Shop Manager—not discussed directly with the upfitters in the heat of the moment.
* **The "Process First" Approach:** When an issue arises, we will address it using a "no-names" approach. We assume the system or SOP failed, not the technician. We analyze the problem and fix the process first. If the process is fixed and the issue persists, *then* it is handled as a personnel issue.

## 5. Software Deliverables: What UpfittersOS Will Handle
As part of this role, I will continue building and managing the floor using UpfittersOS. The software will directly handle:

* **Roles & Responsibilities Layout:** Detailed job descriptions and SOPs clearly defined within the platform so every team member knows exactly what is expected.
* **Parts Request Hub:** Techs submit digital parts requests and check statuses from their bay terminals, routing all requests directly to the Manager.
* **Efficiency Reporting:** Automated generation of efficiency reports tracking:
    * Techs' actual time vs. booked time
    * Operational holdups and missed deadline reasons
    * QC failure rates and kickback logs
    * Parts delays and inventory issues
